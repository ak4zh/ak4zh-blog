---
title: The Peak
date: 2026-11-26
---

When I am in the mountains, I just look at the peaks and think about how it was not always there. Millions of years ago something began to rise, slowly, under pressure and friction which I was not there to see. Layer by layer, it became what it is. By the time I opened my eyes, it was already there.


In a big family, among all the cousins there is often one person who becomes that peak for everyone else. They become someone everyone respects and can rely on. We look up at them the way we look up at the mountains. And like with the mountains, we just see its existence.  


Then one day the peak is gone. You stand there looking at an empty sky, and you feel lost. You don't know where to look anymore.  

A peak is not built overnight, it takes years of inconvenience, resistance and patience. By being there even when its easier not to. By always showing up for others with a smile. Each small act is a layer on top of the last one. A peak that rises that high can be seen from far away, by people we never knew were looking.  


I don't have an answer for this loss. But I have started to believe that a mountain does not simply vanish. What he built does not crumble with him. It stays in how we carry things, in how we show up for each other, and in who we become because we once looked up at him.  


To anyone reading who is standing under the same empty sky: it's okay to feel lost. Keep looking up. Somewhere in us, the stone is still rising, slowly, with friction, one layer at a time. Maybe in our own way, we become a small piece of the peak he was for us.
